# ADR 0005: Permanent bottle identity

- **Status:** Accepted — confirmed by owner 2026-10-10
- **Deciders:** AYVO OS lead architect

## Context

Every physical bottle passes through many fill batches, crates, deliveries,
hotels, locations, cities, regions, countries, returns, wash cycles and
inspections. The system must later reconstruct exactly where a bottle has
been and when. Guests may scan a bottle's QR code to see a public bottle
passport.

## Decision

- Every bottle has **one permanent internal identity** (primary key,
  generated by the system, e.g. UUIDv7) that never changes.
- Every bottle has **one permanent, unique public QR identity** linked 1:1
  to the internal identity. The internal ID and the public QR identity are
  **separate values** (confirmed); the QR identity is never derived from the
  internal ID.
- Identities are **never reused**, including after a bottle is retired or
  destroyed. A retired bottle's identity remains in the database forever,
  with its history.
- The internal ID is not printed on the bottle and is not exposed publicly;
  the QR identity is the external handle.
- The public QR identity must be **opaque and practically unguessable**
  (confirmed): knowing one code must not let anyone guess other valid codes
  (e.g. sufficient random entropy rather than sequential numbers).
- **Public QR URLs remain stable for the physical lifetime of the bottle**
  (confirmed), including after retirement or disposal. URL and domain
  choices must therefore be made with long-term stability in mind.
- Public lookup (bottle passport) is read-only, rate-limited, and returns
  only explicitly approved public information — never hotel names,
  customer data or internal locations unless that is explicitly decided.
- Bottle, crate and other asset QR codes share the same principles; the
  scanner can tell from the code (or a lookup) which asset type it is.
- Handling of a damaged/unreadable QR label (relabelling) is modelled as an
  explicit, audited event, never as silent replacement of the identity.

## Consequences

### Positive

- Complete, unambiguous traceability per bottle.
- Safe public passport: no enumeration of the bottle fleet.
- Simple joins: everything references the stable internal ID.

### Negative

- QR codes must be generated, printed and registered before use; this needs
  a provisioning process.
- Relabelling requires explicit rules and an audit trail.

## Alternatives considered

- **Sequential serial numbers as QR content** — rejected for public lookup
  (enumerable), though a human-readable short code may still be useful
  alongside the QR.
- **Identity per fill cycle** — rejected: breaks lifecycle continuity.

## Open questions (do not assume)

- QR payload format: raw code vs URL, the public domain/path used in the
  URL, length, character set, check digit, human-readable fallback.
- What the public passport shows for a retired or disposed bottle.
- Who prints QR labels, and whether codes are pre-generated in batches.
- Physical labelling method and durability through washing.
- Whether a bottle can ever be relabelled with a new QR code, and if so,
  whether the old code stays resolvable.
- Exactly which fields are public on the bottle passport.
